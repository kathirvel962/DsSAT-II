3. KEY EVIDENCE-BASED INSIGHTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Promotion Stagnation Drives Voluntary Turnover:
   - Employees who were not promoted in the last 2 years experienced an attrition rate of 16.34% (99 exits out of 606).
   - Promoted employees experienced an attrition rate of only 7.80% (28 exits out of 359).
   - Unpromoted employees have more than DOUBLE the turnover rate (2.1x).
   - Statistical Significance: Chi-Square = 13.64, p = 0.00022, df = 1.
   - Machine Learning Confirmation: Promotion status ranks among the top categorical predictors in the Random Forest model.

2. Overwork and Career Lifecycle Inflection Point:
   - Departing employees worked 44.6% more monthly overtime (Mean = 12.15h vs 8.40h for stayers, p = 0.0101; Median = 9.70h vs 8.00h, Mann-Whitney U = 69,106, p = 0.00022).
   - Attrition peaks sharply between years 2 and 4 ("the 3-year itch"), with leavers exhibiting significantly lower tenure (Mean = 3.48y vs 4.71y, p = 7.65e-09; Median = 3.20y vs 4.10y).
   - Departures are concentrated in frontline customer and quota-driven units: Customer Support (19.35%) and Sales (16.67%), compared to Finance (5.69%).

5. STRATEGIC HR RECOMMENDATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Workload Governance in High-Overtime Units:
   - Establish monthly overtime ceilings (e.g. 15 hours) and burnout alert notifications in Customer Support and Sales.
   - Address systemic understaffing rather than relying on chronic employee overtime.

2. Mid-Tenure Promotion Reviews:
   - Introduce structured career development milestones at 2.5 to 3.5 years of tenure to counteract the "3-year itch".
   - Transparent progression tracks can reduce flight risk by over 50% according to observed data.

3. Early-Warning Pulse Interventions:
   - Integrate pulse survey scores (< 3.0) and predictive flight-risk scores (> 60%) into managerial retention alerts.
   - Conduct structured "stay-interviews" with identified at-risk employees before formal resignations occur.
